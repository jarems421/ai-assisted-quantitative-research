# Five investigations: constraints that change who gains or loses

**2026-10-20.** These are the strongest five ideas after the [twenty-candidate search](expanded_discovery_map.md), not five successful source gates. Their distinguishing questions concern response capacity, payment incentives, legal permission, manufacturing qualification and creditor protection. Each could change the sign or distribution of an economic shock. No data panel, signal, return or effect was computed. [Recommendation](expanded_discovery_report.md) · [Evidence record](expanded_discovery_evidence.md).

## F01. Salmon treatment capacity and cross-owner exposure

### The surprising economic connection

Two farmers with comparable lice pressure need not have comparable near-term risk. One may control suitable treatment capacity; the other may depend on vessels simultaneously needed by neighboring competitors. A biological shock can raise the sector's output price while leaving the second farmer with disproportionate treatment expense, fish loss or early harvesting. The proposed relationship is **rivals' demand for a shared response resource → focal firm's inability to mitigate → asymmetric cash-flow risk**. It is not simply lice counts predicting salmon prices.

The first operational hypothesis would be: conditional on own-site health, temperature, recent treatment and local biological pressure, unusually simultaneous treatment needs at *other owners' sites sharing a previously evidenced service pool* predict delayed mitigation or continued high burden over the next 1–4 weeks. The eventual investment hypothesis is a weaker relative outlook for firms whose exposed sites lack protected response capacity, rather than a blanket short of salmon producers. Neither hypothesis has been tested; the sign of aggregate equity exposure is not predetermined.

### Exact sources and feasible historical question

BarentsWatch's [Fish Health guide](https://www.barentswatch.no/en/tutorials/fish-health/) describes weekly locality reports, measures and historical register information from 2012. Reports arrive in arrears, generally by Tuesday of the following week. It also warns that a zero in one export can represent either a reported zero or an empty field: that projection cannot certify absence. Its [December 2020 release](https://www.barentswatch.no/en/articles/updates-in-the-fishhealth-service/) separately dates visible vessel tracks to week 35/2020. **The health series' start is not the vessel series' start.**

The proposed join is locality ID/year/week → treatments/health → vessel visits and time → dated permit holder → listed parent. Candidate issuers are Mowi (MOWI), SalMar (SALM) and Lerøy (LSG), with historical class/ownership checks deferred. Capacity licences are not observed fish biomass. A boat's visit is not a treatment record; some visits move smolts or harvested fish, and AIS presence does not reveal spare treatment slots. The [API documentation](https://developer.barentswatch.no/docs/fishhealth/) requires registration/terms review and distinguishes generally open data from commercially licensed `v1/shipinfo`. No endpoint or account was exercised. Historical task labels, compatible vessel characteristics and use rights remain decisive uncertainties.

As an economic anchor, [Lerøy's 2024 report](https://www.leroyseafood.com/globalassets/02--documents/english/annual-reports/lsg-annual-report-2024.pdf) links treatment handling with welfare/mortality risk. A dated [Måsøval announcement](https://www.masoval.no/announcements/masoval-as-increases-wellboat-capacity/) describes a subsidiary's time charter for added capacity; its [2025 commissioning account](https://www.masoval.no/en/artikler/njord-kya-christened-at-siholmen-strengthens-wellboat-capacity-at-masoval/) shows that this is an actual investment/contracting decision, not an invented resource constraint. These are examples, not a certified charter inventory or future study sample.

It is nearby financial prior art, not a replicated trading result. Thus **neither biological risk nor its financial relevance is claimed new**.

### Where an advantage could persist—and how it fails

The proposed friction is split expertise: biological models describe infection, ship operators allocate vessels, while equity analysis aggregates company costs. A time-varying service dependency may cross those boundaries. This is a hypothesis about integration, not evidence that specialists ignore it. A simple public boat map could be inferior to private charter knowledge.

Falsify the difference if the service measure merely repeats local infection, weather or own treatment persistence; if results depend on future visits to infer today's network; if all boats are treated as interchangeable; or if removing own-company demand eliminates the relationship. A placebo service link to a non-sharing area should not predict the focal farm after biological controls. Weather, fish size, gill disease, treatment choice, wellboat infection transmission and private information can confound congestion. More treatment is not necessarily better welfare, so reduced treatment cannot automatically be labeled a failure.

A later Level A analysis could assess reconstructed resource constraints; Level B would require evidence for when each report and mapping was public. An actionable 5–20-session equity design would additionally need a separately declared outcome, sector-price/currency/risk comparison, liquidity/costs and independent evaluation. Overlapping site-weeks and a few issuers are not independent investment observations. **Verdict: most compelling next source-verification direction, conditional on observing legally usable service dependencies. No generic lice-count fallback is proposed.**

## F02. Provider payment wedges and device demand

### The mechanism is a transfer through someone else's margin

A manufacturer's product may remain clinically covered while the professional performing the procedure loses separate payment for a complementary service. The hospital can remain adequately reimbursed, yet a physician's incentive or billing uncertainty can slow adoption. A coverage-only analysis can therefore predict the wrong demand response. Conversely, billing education or an allowed modifier may remove the apparent problem without any change in clinical coverage.

The concrete hypothesis is: a newly binding, non-bypassable same-episode payment edit affecting a required complement predicts a worse supplier procedure/adoption outlook than the same coverage policy without that payment wedge. The relevant difference is **which party must provide the complementary activity and absorbs the lost payment**, not a different lag on a coverage announcement. INSP is an illustrative focused manufacturer; a company must supply a dated original procedure/coding bridge before entering any eventual sample.

### Sources, timing and evidence

CMS [PTP edit files](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coding-billing/national-correct-coding-initiative-ncci-edits/medicare-ncci-procedure-procedure-ptp-edits) identify code pairs, settings and quarterly changes, with posted and effective dates. The [FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coding-billing/national-correct-coding-initiative-ncci-edits/medicare-ncci-faq-library) explains modifier indicators and historical effective/deletion information. Not every edit forbids separate payment in every clinical circumstance, and not every code pair is actually co-provided. Current historical fields do not recover every originally published version.

The [Medicare Coverage Database](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/downloads/downloads.aspx) places much code information in articles rather than LCDs; downloads contain latest versions, with other versions/API/archive handled separately. Proposed joins are original article/LCD ID and version → exact code pair/setting → issuer's contemporaneous billing guide → provider economic role. Fee schedules and a public case explaining the episode are needed before estimating a payment difference. CPT licence terms constrain reuse; a code's availability on a page is not blanket redistribution permission.

There is direct economic, rather than merely theoretical, evidence: [NeoGenomics' 2014 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1077183/000119312514396273/d775377d10q.htm) reports a revenue reduction associated with its conservative interpretation of NCCI edits. An [Inspire 2026 presentation filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1609550/000160955026000035/wellsfargoinnovationspot.htm) distinguishes coding, coverage, facility payment and physician payment and discusses education. These disclosed cases make the transmission credible and show that it is already discussed with investors. They are not untouched confirmation, event-return results or proof that our narrower hypothesis works.

### Exact prior-art comparison

| Prior work / offering | Already supplied | What would have to be additional |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moneer et al., [Health Affairs 2024](https://doi.org/10.1377/hlthaff.2024.00182) | Studies evidence behind local coverage decisions in 2015–2022. | A payment-incentive channel between clinical eligibility and supplier adoption; the paper is not a test of that equity prediction. Abstract reviewed; no subscription/full-text claim. |
| [AAPC Codify](https://lp.aapc.com/codify/features.aspx) | Already combines edit checks, LCD lookup, cross-references and claim scrubbing. | **Joining coverage and edits is not novel.** The difference would have to be supplier-specific exposure to the provider margin wedge and its incremental interpretation, not another coding tool. |
| [MMIT](https://www.mmitnetwork.com/) | Markets coverage, payer/provider data and reimbursement/access intelligence. | Compare against actual policy/claims context before claiming neglected economics. Advertised scope does not prove funds trade this exact rule, but it is a serious substitute. |
| Original issuer investor disclosures | Explain economically important coding questions directly. | An omitted second-order complement or exposure asymmetry, not a trade on the already-public headline case. |

### Testable path and failure conditions

A later test could contrast suppliers with a documented binding complement against covered products not requiring that complement, using only rules posted before the response window. The natural information clock is the first evidenced posting, not the future effective date. A 1–4-week interpretation/adoption response is plausible but unmeasured. Actual utilization may appear only in delayed claims or company reports; do not force a weekly revenue target. Market reactions can be the eventual direct outcome after Level B timing is established.

Fail the idea if clinical modifiers neutralize the supposed wedge, if manufacturer discounts already absorb it, if professional fees are immaterial to adoption, if no public source establishes co-provision, or if expert investor communications already contain the complete inference. Quarterly edit releases create shared shocks; thousands of code pairs do not supply thousands of independent events. **Verdict: second choice for economic importance and identifiable public rules; substantial interpretation and commercial competition, not a demonstrated unexplored signal.**

## F03. Authorisation chains and substitution option value

### A positive decision can be negative for a different company

Suppose an industrial process uses a restricted chemical. A qualified substitute supplier may expect new business if users must convert. A permission allowing the old use to continue can remove that switching catalyst—even while benefiting the downstream manufacturer and incumbent formulator. A generic hazardous-substance exposure score cannot tell those companies apart.

The hypothesis is: relative to a broad authorisation headline, the interaction of the **specific permitted use, covered supply chain and already-qualified replacement process** predicts opposite expectation changes for incumbents and substitution suppliers. The decision changes an option to defer a conversion, not merely an environmental label. The potentially overlooked information is which contractual/technical pathway remains usable, including whether a customer's exact use is inside an upstream supplier's permission.

### Exact documents and exposure evidence

[ECHA's application guidance](https://echa.europa.eu/en-GB/web/guest/applying-for-authorisation/start-preparing-your-application) describes analyses of alternatives, socio-economic effects and substitution plans. These can identify why a technically possible alternative is not economically feasible for a particular use. The authoritative decision text and relevant regulation, rather than a database label or this research note, determine scope.

[IAEG's aerospace table](https://www.iaeg.com/workgroups/wg5/authorisation) lists chromate substances, uses, holders and authorisation references, including PPG-related entities and aerospace supply chains. [An ECHA-indexed application listing](https://echa.europa.eu/es/applications-for-authorisation-previous-consultations?_viewsubstances_WAR_echarevsubstanceportlet_cur=3&_viewsubstances_WAR_echarevsubstanceportlet_delta=50&_viewsubstances_WAR_echarevsubstanceportlet_orderByCol=synonymDynamicField_1512&_viewsubstances_WAR_echarevsubstanceportlet_orderByType=asc&_viewsubstances_WAR_echarevsubstanceportlet_resetCur=false&p_p_id=viewsubstances_WAR_echarevsubstanceportlet&p_p_lifecycle=0&p_p_mode=view&p_p_state=normal) identifies MTU Aero Engines and a use-specific application. These demonstrate that listed-entity bridges can exist; they do not certify material product revenue, every historical corporate transfer or the identity of a winning substitute vendor. MKS/Atotech would need a dated acquisition/subsidiary bridge.

Potential historical records include applications, consultation materials, committee opinions, final Commission decisions, corrections and reviews. Preserve each as a separate version/event state; a final decision can be fully anticipated. The one selected ECHA decision PDF returned browser 403 here and was not rerouted or retried. Its indexed text is not a fully inspected original. The legal and historical source chain remains incomplete.

### Why this could be differentiated, and why it may not be investable

The proposed edge would come from a *reversal of who benefits*, supported by exact use and qualification evidence. It is not another threshold on chemical exposure. A possible delayed response concerns a small supplier whose substitution story changes indirectly through a customer's upstream authorisation, rather than a prominently named applicant. That path must be documented before calling it overlooked.

Falsify the idea if the substitute was never technically/customer-qualified; the extension was already expected; the affected use is immaterial; contracts guarantee the order regardless; or the relevant benefit was disclosed in investor material. Transition periods, review applications, legal challenges and alternative supply chains can prevent an apparent cliff. Many economic effects take years, so only an actual news revision—not a known sunset countdown—fits a 1–4-week market study. **Verdict: third choice, potentially important interpretation asymmetry, with harder materiality/legal-source gates than F01/F02. No legal or trading conclusion is certified.**

## F04. Pharmaceutical input qualification bottlenecks

### Why supplier counts can be misleading

A medicine with several API certificates may still rely on one physical site or manufacturing step. Conversely, a site restriction may leave particular products exempt or permit continued supply to avoid shortages. A rival with a certificate is not necessarily qualified in the affected finished-dose product, nor able to increase output. The proposed relationship is **loss of a common qualified production option → uneven replacement ability across listed firms**, not simply bad inspection news predicting bad stock returns.

The concrete hypothesis would distinguish an affected API with independent, already-evidenced replacement routes from an apparently diversified API whose certificates share a restricted dependency. Subsequent product availability or disclosed volume/margin changes could test the mechanism; an eventual 5–20-session market study would ask whether the exposure read-through arrives after the headline. Dr. Reddy's, Hikma, Teva and Sandoz are possible parent examples; none is assigned an event or directional signal here.

### What public sources really provide

[EudraGMDP](https://eudragmdp.ema.europa.eu/) contains manufacturing authorisations, certificates and non-compliance statements, with some sensitive information withheld. Its [EMA history](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/research-development/compliance-research-development/good-manufacturing-practice/eudragmdp-database?page=1) describes non-compliance functionality introduced in 2009. Absence of a certificate does not mean non-compliance.

[EDQM's current action page](https://www.edqm.eu/en/actions-on-ceps) gives dated substance/CEP examples and separates voluntary inability to produce, GMP-related action and certification failures. It explicitly presents six months of updates; that is not an exhaustive long historical event tape. Its [FAQ](https://faq.edqm.eu/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=1377010) describes latest-valid certificate and invalidation information. [Inspection guidance](https://www.edqm.eu/en/the-inspection-programme) explains that action can affect a site within a multi-manufacturer certificate. A CEP event therefore needs its actual scope, reason and product consequences; do not infer a universal manufacturing ban.

The [EMA manufacturing-shortage reflection paper](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/other/reflection-paper-medicinal-product-supply-shortages-caused-manufacturing-good-manufacturing-practice-compliance-problems_en.pdf) supports the supply-risk mechanism. *Generic Drug Shortage in Japan: GMP Noncompliance and Associated Quality Issues*, [Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences, 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S002235492300103X), is neighboring original research, not evidence about our specific European cross-firm prediction.

### Commercial comparison and barriers

[Clarivate's release schedule](https://clarivate.com/life-sciences-healthcare/training-support/cortellis/product-intelligence-release-information/) specifically lists EudraGMDP manufacturing/registration information, EDQM CEPs, API availability/manufacturing status, subsidiary data and consumption/sales sources. Its [Product Intelligence description](https://clarivate.com/life-sciences-healthcare/zh/manufacturing-supply-chain-intelligence/product-intelligence-analytics/) markets sourcing, competition and supply-chain uses. This is a much closer substitute than a generic news feed. Publicly reconstructing a manufacturer list would not be meaningful differentiation.

The possible addition is a product-specific *replacement constraint*, established independently of certificate counts and combined with financial materiality. Yet customers, formulation specifications, validated sites, actual capacity and supply contracts can be confidential. Private procurement data may dominate any free-data model. Duplicate actions across certificates/sites and regulator exceptions reduce effective sample size. No independent historical event count or feasible public customer matrix was established.

Fail if the inference requires unavailable formulation/qualification files, if all prospective effects are already in shortage notices, or if the putative beneficiary shares the same input dependency. **Verdict: economically important but fourth choice: exact commercial overlap and missing product/customer qualification evidence prevent promotion.** This is a new European input-manufacturing question, not a rescue of the abandoned FDA decision-universe frame; no FDA request or acquisition occurred.

## F09. Creditor protection and supplier cash risk

### A filing changes the meaning of the gross receivable

The same customer failure can be a revenue-loss story, a liquidity shock, or largely insured accounting exposure. A court order permitting payments to a critical supplier can preserve cash even when a creditor schedule initially looks alarming. Conversely, the later discovery of an unprotected claim can expose a listed supplier not visible in conventional major-customer disclosures. The proposed variable is **newly evidenced unprotected cash exposure relative to the supplier's liquidity**, not the debtor's bankruptcy indicator.

A later hypothesis would compare suppliers with independently documented payment protection against otherwise similar documented claims without that protection. The information event would be the first public docket material establishing the distinction, not necessarily the petition date. Supplier shares, receivables insurance, collateral, disputed claims and cash already recovered must remain separate. A schedule is neither an adjudication nor a loss estimate. Avnet/Arrow or other distributors could enter only if a source actually names and legally links them; their inclusion here is illustrative.

### Source history and close competition

[RECAP](https://courtlistener.com/recap) is a contributed archive of federal court records, not a complete public filing census. Case, docket, entry, attachment and upload metadata could support document lineage, but filing time is not proof that the free copy was available then. No PACER account, request, claim register or document corpus was acquired. Free claims-agent pages might help only where the original, publication status and terms are established; missing cases remain missing, not zero exposure.

The [Hertzel/Li/Officer/Rodgers 2008 paper](https://experts.azregents.edu/en/publications/inter-firm-linkages-and-the-wealth-effects-of-financial-distress-/), *Journal of Financial Economics*, directly studies distress wealth effects along supplier/customer links. It already establishes close financial prior art. Our proposed distinction is legal payment protection and hidden cash exposure revealed by subsequent records, not discovery of contagion.

[Octus First Day](https://octus.com/products/first-day-intelligence/) markets alerts and case/creditor summaries; its [Credit Intelligence](https://octus.com/products/credit-intelligence/) explicitly offers unsecured claim nature, amount and status. [BankruptcyObserver's enterprise description](https://www.bankruptcyobserver.com/enterprise) additionally markets matching customer/account identifiers and scanning creditor lists. These offerings directly weaken a claim that finding a creditor name is an overlooked service. Vendor statements establish marketed functions, not audited performance or our access rights. No subscription/trial/contact occurred.

### Remaining possible contribution and verdict

The only defensible difference would be **cross-market materiality and protection**, especially where a small cash-constrained public supplier is incidentally named in a private customer's docket. If a specialist feed already supplies that information promptly, or the supplier has disclosed the loss, no advantage remains. Lawyers and credit investors often understand priorities better than an automated parser; trade-credit insurers may have information unavailable publicly.

A 1–4-week revelation process is conceivable as motions/orders arrive, but customer distress is often anticipated. Later allowance or recovery cannot label a claim as protected at the earlier date. Claim duplicates, amendments, subsidiaries, currencies, contingent amounts, cancelled purchase orders and personal data create substantial interpretation risk. A useful cash-risk case study may lack enough independent events for a strategy. **Verdict: fifth choice and deliberately demoted after finding close substitutes.** The research would need a distinctive protection/materiality inference, not another court-alert pipeline.

## Cross-candidate conclusion

All five use known sources and established economic ingredients. Their proposed differences are the identities of the constrained actor, the feasible response set and who ultimately receives or loses cash. Those differences are specific enough to falsify; none establishes global originality or a tradable advantage. The search did not discover a verified untouched niche. It did identify three materially different questions worth assessing before another hypothesis is selected. No numerical effect, profitability estimate or selected holding rule follows from this review.
