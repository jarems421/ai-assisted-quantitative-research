## 2. The SH09 hypothesis

Some offshore oil and gas assets carry decommissioning and abandonment obligations that may remain enforceable against former owners.

When an operator enters bankruptcy, assets that were expected to be sold to a solvent successor may instead be abandoned or fail to attract a buyer.

That may increase the expected financial burden borne by a predecessor company.

The investment-relevant hypothesis is not simply:

"Offshore bankruptcies create cleanup liabilities."

That mechanism is already known to legal specialists, companies and regulators.

The distinctive potential information advantage would be:

**A researcher reconstructs a material change in a listed former owner's recoverable-net cleanup exposure from public bankruptcy, lease, engineering-cost and financial-security information before the listed company communicates the resulting financial impact.**

The intended investment horizon is approximately 5–20 trading sessions.

However, this horizon only makes sense if there is an identifiable information event that may change investor expectations before an issuer announcement or another obvious competing disclosure.

We must establish that timing rather than assume it.

## 4. The central verification question

Determine whether the information necessary to reconstruct an economically meaningful change in Chevron's predecessor liability was publicly obtainable **before the 2 January 2024 issuer disclosure**.

Separate the question into four parts:

### A. Historical asset status

Was there an original, publicly available bankruptcy filing, motion, order or attachment identifying the specific assets or leases that were expected to be sold but were subsequently abandoned or left without a buyer?

Establish:

- Original document.
- Filing date.
- Evidence of public availability.
- Identifiable lease/asset population.
- Relevant sale or abandonment status.
- Whether the document existed publicly before 2 January 2024.

A later description of an earlier filing is not equivalent to acquiring the actual earlier filing.

### B. Chevron's predecessor obligation

Could public records available at the relevant historical time establish Chevron's legally relevant predecessor interest in the identified assets?

Distinguish:

- Former ownership.
- Lease or assignment history.
- Obligations that accrued during the relevant tenure.
- Operator versus non-operator responsibility.
- Other potentially responsible parties.
- Applicable liability rules.

A matching historical company name is not sufficient evidence of financial responsibility.

### C. Financial magnitude and recoverable security

Could the same historical information establish an interpretable financial exposure?

Investigate whether public records then provided:

- Dated lease-level or asset-level decommissioning cost estimates.
- Applicable financial assurance.
- Evidence of which assets the assurance covered.
- Relevant recoverability restrictions.
- Identified predecessor liabilities.
- Sufficient information to distinguish gross engineering costs from potentially retained economic exposure.

Preserve estimate ranges, accounting bases and joint-liability uncertainty.

**Do not calculate a pseudo-net liability by mechanically subtracting the full $170 million security amount or allocating it proportionally across leases.**

If coverage or recoverability cannot be established, mark net exposure unverified.

Current BSEE cost estimates cannot automatically substitute for historical 2023 estimates.

### D. Information increment

If the historical records exist, determine what genuinely new information they supplied compared with:

- Chevron's contemporaneous disclosures.
- Known bankruptcy information.
- Existing industry and legal analysis.
- Specialist offshore decommissioning information services.
- Already established financial obligations.

Investigate whether a reasonably informed analyst could have identified a *change* in expected exposure that was not already obvious.

Do not infer that a profitable market inefficiency existed merely because a calculation was legally complicated.

## 5. Research procedure

Follow this sequence.

**Step 1 — Historical publication check**

Identify the original December 2023 sale/abandonment filing and relevant attachment using legitimate free public sources.

Establish actual availability, not merely a document date.

If the necessary filing is behind paid PACER access, an authentication restriction or another unavailable source, stop that route.

Do not purchase access, create an account or circumvent restrictions.

**Step 2 — Deterministic small-sample mapping**

Only if the original asset list is found, select at most two relevant lease IDs in ascending canonical identifier order before detailed matching.

For these selected examples, attempt to reconstruct:

- Asset identity.
- Sale/abandonment status.
- Historical Chevron predecessor interest.
- Relevant historical obligation.
- Dated engineering-cost estimate.
- Applicable financial security.
- Historical public availability.

Record each field as:

- VERIFIED AS-OF
- VERIFIED ONLY RETROSPECTIVELY
- UNVERIFIED
- CONTRADICTED

Two leases can demonstrate whether the original records are practically joinable.

They cannot, by themselves, establish Chevron's aggregate economic exposure or an investment forecast.

**Step 3 — Independent outcome route**

Identify how a future Level A study could verify the consequences using a correctly matched outcome.

Consider:

- Issuer liability revisions.
- Subsequent provisions.
- Asset-specific regulatory cost estimates.
- Actual payments or recoveries where independently recorded.

Do not assign Chevron's total Cox/Fieldwood-related financial charge to the two sampled leases.

The historical outcome is a verification target, not a permission to reverse-engineer an apparently accurate forecast.

**Step 4 — Competitive and timing assessment**

Compare the information with the closest contemporaneous public or specialist interpretation.

Assess what a competent specialist likely knew.

Identify the proposed event clock and why an economically meaningful expectation revision might occur over 5–20 trading sessions.

Do not look at historical equity returns to justify this argument.

## 8. Decision criteria

At completion, assign one of the following evidence-grounded decisions.

### STOP — Historical information not reconstructable

Essential predictor information, liability mapping, security allocation or required source vintages cannot be established within the bounded public verification.

State whether this is a genuine documented impossibility, an access limit or simply an unresolved question.

Do not claim that all future versions of the idea are impossible.

### MEASURABLE, NOT HISTORICALLY ACTIONABLE

A retrospective economic object can be reconstructed, but the required information was unavailable, unverifiable or insufficiently interpreted before the company disclosed it.

Do not promote this as a short-horizon trading opportunity.

### M PASSED, H UNCONVINCING

The original measurement is feasible, but there is no concrete near-term expectation-revision mechanism or meaningful increment over existing disclosures and specialists.

Do not recommend an investment pilot.

### VERIFY — One decisive remaining question

Most requirements are evidenced, but one precisely identified and inexpensive uncertainty remains.

Propose the smallest possible additional check.

Do not execute it outside this authorisation.

### LEVEL A DESIGN CANDIDATE — Requires separate approval

Only if the evidence genuinely establishes measurement feasibility, a credible independent economic outcome route, meaningful exposure, worthwhile population design, and a justified investment-relevance proposition should you propose a later Level A design.

Do not launch it.

Importantly, M passing on two sampled leases is not enough to establish population coverage or a full-company valuation bridge.
