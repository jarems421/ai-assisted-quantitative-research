## 3. Change the selection process: evidence of the distinctive variable first

For every candidate, identify the exact variable, relationship or inference that would make the idea different from existing approaches.

Then ask:

**What original historical evidence would actually establish that variable?**

Do this before recommending a full research pilot.

I want you to distinguish five separate stages:

1. **Economic mechanism:** Why should this real-world phenomenon affect costs, demand, competition, cash flows or risk?
2. **Observable information:** Which dated records reveal something about that mechanism?
3. **Distinctive inference:** What can we establish that simpler datasets, obvious signals and commercial substitutes may miss?
4. **Financial materiality:** Why could the information meaningfully affect a listed business?
5. **Potential market inefficiency:** Why might investors incorporate that information imperfectly or with a delay?

The first two are not enough.

A convincing story is not enough.

An unusual source is not enough.

A difficult data integration problem is not automatically an information advantage.

**The strongest candidate is one where all five stages have a credible evidence path.**

You do not need to prove profitability at this stage, but you must identify what evidence could eventually support or reject it.

## 5. Introduce an observability audit

For the strongest candidates, create an explicit chain of necessary evidence.

For example:

Original event → affected economic relationship → identifiable listed business → measurable outcome → plausible market-information timing.

Mark each link as:

- **VERIFIED:** Supported by inspected original evidence.
- **PLAUSIBLE:** Economically reasonable, but not directly established.
- **MISSING:** Necessary information has not been found.
- **INACCESSIBLE:** Available evidence suggests a legal, practical or cost barrier.
- **NOT REQUIRED:** The link is unnecessary for the proposed narrower hypothesis.

Be conservative.

Do not classify a supplier as exposed merely because it sells a relevant product.

Do not classify a company as financially material merely because its name appears in a regulatory document.

Do not infer historic relationships from current ownership without a dated bridge.

Do not reconstruct booking rights, contracts, qualifications or revenue commitments from weak proxies and claim they are observed.

Crucially, avoid hypotheses where **the entire investment advantage depends on a private variable we have no credible way to reconstruct**.

Such opportunities may be economically fascinating, but they are unsuitable for our present public-data research programme.

Do not assume that no published paper means nobody exploits the relationship.

## 10. Explicitly separate exploration, historical actionability and profitability

Maintain our existing evidence framework.

**Level A:** A retrospective economic relationship or forecast comparison, with all reconstruction and selection limitations disclosed.

**Level B:** Evidence that the relevant information existed and was publicly available at the historical decision point.

**Level C:** Execution-aware evidence incorporating actual market implementation, costs, liquidity and independent evaluation.

A Level A association is not an investment edge.

But a Level A study does not need a complete execution ledger or proof of every historical trading condition before it can teach us something useful.

Do not make unsupported causal claims.

Do not turn retrospectively reconstructed information into a historically tradeable signal.

Do not p-hack or repeatedly alter signals after inspecting results.

A negative or inconclusive economic result is useful if the underlying measurement is valid.
