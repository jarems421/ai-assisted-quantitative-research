# Frontier Selection II: real economics, no differentiated pilot ready

**2026-10-20 — Recommend NEITHER for an economic pilot.** F02 establishes a financially relevant provider-incentive mechanism, but its strongest example is already explicitly interpreted by the manufacturer and closely studied academically. F03 establishes real commercial substitutes and use-specific legal permissions, but not a qualified substitute's material sales opportunity affected by the decision. The distinction we wanted to measure remains missing. No economic experiment or stock-return analysis ran.

## F02: economically important, already interpreted

### Inspire: coverage and clinical dependence are observable; a negative wedge is not

Inspire's original 2021 filing identifies drug-induced sleep endoscopy (DISE) as the final patient-selection procedure and separates implantation, professional services and bundled hospital/ambulatory-surgical-centre payments. Its reported 2022 national averages are approximately **$888 for implantation, $115 for DISE, $30,063 for the hospital and $24,828 for the ASC**. These are different recipients and services; they cannot be added into manufacturer revenue or provider profit. Facility payments were described as approximately 2% above 2021. This example did **not** establish a newly defunded necessary complement. [Inspire 2021 filing, pp. 18 and 77](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001609550/c301639a-04ba-4ed5-a000-b73f9f75fd52.pdf).

The same filing reports **$233.4 million FY2021 revenue**, principally from the Inspire system, and says Medicare historically represented approximately **25% of US implants**. Thus reimbursement can matter without every sale being Medicare-dependent. These later financial disclosures assess product dependence; they are not information supplied to an earlier forecast. We did not independently reproduce the fee amounts from matched original CMS schedules, measure actual collections/costs or estimate demand elasticity. [Inspire filing, pp. 18, 37 and 78–81](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001609550/c301639a-04ba-4ed5-a000-b73f9f75fd52.pdf).

The inspected CMS A57149 version 19 is **superseded 2026 text**, containing a revision history for 2021/2022 changes. It confirms the coding story retrospectively, not the exact text publicly served in 2021. Its general coding disclaimer does not certify a particular prohibited code pair. The denied November 2021 issuer release was not recovered or retried. [CMS coding article and revision history](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/article.aspx?articleid=57149&ver=19).

### Glaukos: the proposed interpretation appears in the issuer's own disclosure

Glaukos's 2021 10-K explicitly distinguishes **significantly lower physician payment** for adding a trabecular bypass stent during cataract surgery from a slight ASC facility decrease and hospital facility increase. It estimates roughly **80% ASC / 20% hospital** procedure mix. The company itself connects provider reimbursement uncertainty with competitive product trials and possible procedure/revenue pressure. This is the provider-to-manufacturer inference we sought, already stated publicly; the company's attribution is not an independently established causal effect. [Glaukos 2021 10-K, “2022 U.S. reimbursement rates”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1192448/000155837022002371/gkos-20211231x10k.htm).

Financial relevance is credible: FY2021 sales were **$294.0 million**, including **$170.8 million US glaucoma sales**, while the global iStent platform represented approximately **79% of total sales**. These overlapping business measures must not be combined into an invented affected-revenue figure. Neither is a quantified loss from the rule. Geographic/payer mix, alternative procedures, product competition and pandemic recovery can offset or confound the incentive. [Glaukos 10-K, sales and product-mix disclosures](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1192448/000155837022002371/gkos-20211231x10k.htm).

Independent CMS records also show why a simple effective-date/code flag is inadequate:

- The 2022 PFS landing records **November 2, 2021 display**, **November 19 publication** and January 2022 implementation. Proposed rates had already been discussed in July, according to the issuer. [CMS final-rule record](https://www.cms.gov/medicaremedicare-fee-service-paymentphysicianfeeschedpfs-federal-regulation-notices/cms-1751-f).
- A later correction changes the printed **66991 work RVU from 7.41 to 9.23**, reflecting previously finalised policy. Work RVU is one fee component, not the total payment or a fresh economic policy shock. [Original correction, p. 4](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2022-02623.pdf).
- CMS's April OPPS notice records a separate facility classification correction for 66989/66991, retroactive to January 1. It cannot stand in for the physician-fee change. [CMS MM12666](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/mm12666-april-2022-update-hospital-outpatient-prospective-payment-system-opps.pdf).

The small selected-procedure supplement independently supplies cataract-only 66984 professional payments of **$548.17 for 2021 and $528.84 under the initial 2022 final rule**. It does not supply 66991. The additional selected-code PE supplement also lacks 66991. We therefore **did not reconstruct the full old/new incremental stent fee**. Indexed dollar snippets from the denied issuer call PDF and an academic abstract were not promoted to matched-vintage CMS observations. No tolerance, nationwide/MAC-rate substitution or provider-margin assumption repairs that gap. [CMS selected-procedure supplement](https://www.cms.gov/files/zip/cy-2022-pfs-final-rule-impact-payment-selected-procedures.zip).

No case was certified as a specific non-bypassable NCCI denial. CMS explains that setting, provider/date and modifier eligibility matter; a permitted modifier still requires the clinical circumstances. A combined-code repricing is not automatically an NCCI prohibition. [CMS FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coding-billing/national-correct-coding-initiative-ncci-edits/medicare-ncci-faq-library).

### Competition substantially narrows the proposed advantage

Gulani, Anderson and Johnson's 2026 accepted/in-press paper directly studies Medicare payment changes, competing glaucoma procedures and manufacturer transfers to physicians, using 2018–2023 Medicare claims and Open Payments. Its comparator includes trabeculectomy. That is close prior art for the proposed incentive/substitution relationship, beyond generic coverage research. We inspected the original institutional abstract, not full methods or data, and adopted none of its estimated effects or headline payment amounts as our own. [Original author-institution record](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/comparing-national-microinvasive-glaucoma-surgery-procedure-rates/).

AAPC advertises CCI checks, coverage lookup, historical codes, fee schedules and integrated claim scrubbing. MMIT advertises coverage, payer/provider information and access/utilisation analysis, principally in pharmaceutical market access; its page does not establish this particular device-procedure forecast. Both support external interest, not verified profitable trading or exact historical product capability. [AAPC](https://lp.aapc.com/codify/features.aspx), [MMIT](https://www.mmitnetwork.com/).

The hypothetical remaining difference would be a **previously unrecognised, indispensable low-margin complement**, mapped to a material device franchise before issuer interpretation. Neither fixed example demonstrates it. More parsing of these cases cannot create that difference. A generic reimbursement-volume replication could be useful economics, but would change the intended investment proposition. We do not propose it as the next pilot.

## F03: real substitution, missing qualified sales at risk

### Lead pigments: a genuine commercial alternative, not a measured loser

The Court's own digest describes the 2019 annulment of DCC Maastricht's lead-pigment authorisation for uses including metal paints and road markings. It explains that an alternative must be technically/economically suitable for the applicant, beyond a laboratory possibility. This is later judicial explanation; the original 2016 controlling decision and its first public release were **not recovered**. The separate EUR-Lex judgment request returned an empty HTTP 202 response. [Original Court digest, T-837/16 section](https://curia.europa.eu/common/recdoc/repertoire_jurisp/bull_3/data/3_02_06.htm).

BASF's **February 5, 2015** release announced expansion of bismuth-vanadate pigment capacity, expected available in 2017, and directly cited demand for lead-chromate alternatives. Named products were already commercial. This is evidence of economic motivation and capacity investment before the selected authorisation—not a substitute inferred from chemical similarity. [Original BASF release](https://www.basf.com/dam/jcr%3A4e263579-ff63-36cd-8c7a-0b10347bfe29/basf/www/global/documents/en/news-and-media/news-releases/2015/02/P130e_BASF_bismuth%20vanadate%20pigments.pdf).

It does not identify the relevant customer's qualified formulation, switch contract, expected sales, investment amount or orders lost because incumbent use continued. The release's approximately **€74 billion parent sales for 2013** is corporate context, not pigment exposure. It cannot establish materiality; product-family membership does not establish interchangeable performance for every authorised use. BASF's dated ownership is not projected into current pigment ownership. No issuer loss forecast is constructed.

### Functional chrome: exact permitted properties do not identify substitute revenue

The original **December 23, 2020 Official Journal**, notice **2020/C 447/03**, summarises decision **C(2020)8797 of December 18**. It lists chromium trioxide, seven upstream holders and functional plating uses tied to properties including wear resistance, hardness, layer thickness and corrosion resistance. Thus use-specific permission and legal actors are observable. The retrieved summary is not the full controlling decision or its application/alternatives dossier. Its September 2024 review expiry is not assumed to be a hard ban, nor a statement of present legal status. [Original OJ, printed pp. C447/5–8](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AC%3A2020%3A447%3AFULL).

Bodycote's 2022 aerospace brochure markets HVOF replacement applications for hard chrome, identifies component uses and advertises aerospace accreditations/preferred-supplier status. This supports real capability. It does **not** identify qualification for a particular affected customer's component in December 2020, commercially committed replacement volumes or a counterfactual purchase absent authorisation. The two-year dating gap is consequential. [Bodycote brochure, pp. 2–4](https://www.bodycote.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/coating-solutions-for-the-aerospace-and-defence-industries-2022.pdf).

The pre-decision interim report gives **£306.7 million H1 2020 group revenue**, with Specialist Technologies contributing **27%**. Neither number is HVOF revenue, much less regulatory-switch revenue. The report also documents severe pandemic-related aerospace disruption and the April 2020 Ellison acquisition: competing explanations would overwhelm a naive before/after comparison. Historical product/subsidiary qualification, listing/security identity and decision-sensitive exposure would require more than a present parent-company ticker. [Bodycote interim report, pp. 1 and 3–5](https://www.bodycote.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Bodycote-Interim-Press-Release-2020.pdf).

### What would distinguish this from existing compliance analysis?

IAEG already organises chromate substances, consortia, authorisations and downstream-user conditions. Sphera markets regulatory interpretation, chemical management and supply-chain substance declarations. This is evidence of organised specialist attention; neither inspected page establishes the proposed public-equity sales-disappointment forecast. The previously reviewed Slunge/Andersson/Sterner substitution study is also relevant economic prior art, not a newly replicated result. [IAEG](https://www.iaeg.com/workgroups/wg5/authorisation), [Sphera](https://sphera.com/solutions/product-stewardship/product-compliance-software/), [original research](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.132637).

The remaining proposition is precise: **a decision changes a documented, material, qualified supplier opportunity that investors had reason to expect**. Qualification alone is insufficient; legal continuation need not cancel an environmentally preferable switch, and a substitute supplier may also sell incumbent products. Long consultations, review applications, customer qualification cycles and capacity investment can make the final decision anticipated. The business effect could unfold over years; a 5–20-session test requires independently evidenced surprise and material expectation exposure. Neither case supplies those variables.

The missing records might be public in customer qualification announcements, tender awards or segment/backlog disclosures. No such matched record was established here, and access to private contracts is not assumed. Replacing it with total group sales, patents, product listings or generic chemical-risk scores would remove the hypothesis's distinction. **F03 remains interesting but not implementable as proposed from this evidence.**

## Recommendation and the next decision

**Do not launch either pilot.** F02 is easier to connect to financial scale but fails the present differentiation test. F03 is the more distinctive *hypothesis*, yet its missing customer-qualified, material revenue link is precisely the information that would make it valuable. That is not a reason to run a weaker proxy study.

Evidence that would change this decision is narrow. For F02: a separately selected rule affecting a necessary complement, a reproducible incremental provider-payment consequence, material device exposure and an interpretation demonstrably absent from the closest contemporaneous issuer/clinical substitutes. For F03: an as-of qualified customer/product relationship, quantified conditional orders or a material disclosed substitution opportunity, plus a decision that changes it. Neither requires perfect execution certification for Level A; both require the economic variable itself to exist.

I recommend returning to the remaining discovery shortlist before any economic implementation. The next *selection question* I would put to the owner is F09's distinction between gross creditor claims and actual supplier payment protection relative to liquidity. It has a more direct cash-flow object, but the earlier review already found close Octus/credit-research substitutes. It is **not selected as a pilot**, and no new F09 sources were requested. [Existing F09 assessment](expanded_discovery_deep_dives.md#f09-creditor-protection-and-supplier-cash-risk).

For a separately approved next selection review, budget **two analyst-hours, zero paid cost and no engineering** to decide whether existing public orders/disclosures add a protection/materiality inference beyond the nearest product. Require an explicit dated payment entitlement and material exposure before proposing acquisition. If that distinction is absent, stop; do not build a court-alert pipeline. A subsequent meaningful economic-study budget cannot honestly be fixed until an event frame, outcome access and independent sample are demonstrated. No five-day result or sufficient sample is promised.

No empirical experiment is specified because neither candidate passed. This preserves the full selection history without presenting source access or manufacturer commentary as our own economic finding.
