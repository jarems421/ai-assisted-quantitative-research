Approve a small verification investigation of the salmon idea, not a full economic pilot yet.

I would ask Codex to establish four things:

1. Can we identify vessels actually performing relevant treatments, rather than merely visiting or passing farms?
2. Can we demonstrate that particular treatment resources serve multiple independently owned farms?
3. Can we reconstruct which resources are realistically available to each producer without using future knowledge?
4. Do the necessary data have usable rights, historical coverage and sufficient independent observations?

I'd retain Codex's proposed two-analyst-day ceiling. If these conditions cannot be met, I would not allow it to fall back to generic lice forecasting, because that removes precisely what makes the hypothesis interesting.

The good news is that the healthcare-payment and chemical-authorisation ideas are genuinely worthwhile backups, rather than simply weaker versions of salmon farming.

I also particularly like the broader research insight connecting the leading candidates:

The market may understand that an event has occurred, yet misunderstand who can respond to it, who has the contractual right to benefit, and who ultimately bears the cost.

That's a coherent potential research specialism for Research OS. It could lead us toward original cross-industry methods rather than a collection of unrelated obscure-data experiments.

My verdict: this is our strongest discovery round so far. Salmon deserves a tightly controlled source-verification attempt, while healthcare reimbursement deserves serious consideration if that fails.

For the first time, I think we've identified several hypotheses where the economic interpretation itself could be meaningfully differentiated. We haven't demonstrated an advantage yet, but these are worth investigating.
