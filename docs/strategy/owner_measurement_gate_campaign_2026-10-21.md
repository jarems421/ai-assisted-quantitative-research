# 1. Our ultimate objective

Research OS exists to identify potentially exploitable investment information that conventional market participants may not have fully incorporated.

Our preferred investment horizon is approximately **5–20 trading days**, although the underlying economic effects may unfold over longer periods.

We are interested in:

- Unconventional public or legitimately accessible datasets.
- Surprising relationships between independent sources.
- Economic constraints, dependencies, substitution possibilities, rights, obligations and capacity limitations.
- Information that may reveal changes in a company's future revenue, costs, production, margins, liabilities, cash flow or expected performance.
- Mechanisms where one piece of information has a meaningful implication for another company that may not be immediately obvious.
- Potential opportunities suitable for a disciplined retail investor with comparatively small capital.

We do **not** want:

- Generic technical indicators.
- Familiar factor strategies repackaged as novel research.
- Obscure data selected only because it is obscure.
- Industry-wide correlations without distinctive company-specific information.
- Tenuous narratives that require unmeasurable assumptions.
- Reconstructed historical data presented as historically actionable information without proof.
- Mechanisms that take years to affect business outcomes unless there is a specific, measurable near-term expectation-revision event.
- Strategies that are only theoretically profitable before spreads, slippage, market impact and other implementation costs.

Academic or commercial research on a topic does not automatically disqualify it. Such research may validate economic importance.

However, investigate the **closest existing inference or signal**, not merely whether anyone has used the same raw dataset.

Our objective is not necessarily to discover something nobody has ever considered. It is to find a **specific, economically important inference that may remain incompletely incorporated into prices and that we can measure reliably**.

There is currently no evidence that Research OS has discovered a profitable investment strategy.

Do not imply otherwise.

# 4. First objective: improve discovery methodology

The independent review identified a likely structural weakness:

> Research OS has historically developed economically interesting mechanisms before proving that the distinctive information required to investigate them is actually observable.

Evaluate this diagnosis against our existing work.

If supported, implement a narrowly scoped improvement to the discovery process.

The proposed approach is:

**Economic sketch → exact distinctive variable → original-source measurement control → matched outcome → financial materiality → information timing → investment horizon → closest competition → deeper economic research.**

This should replace the tendency to conduct lengthy economic and theoretical investigations before verifying that the key variable exists.

## Introduce Measurement Gate M

Evaluate and, if justified, formally document a new **pre-Level-A Measurement Gate M**.

It must not be confused with a fourth evidence level.

A candidate passes M only when there is a real historical example establishing that:

1. The **distinctive predictor** is genuinely observable, rather than merely narratively plausible.
2. The predictor is recorded in usable and correctly interpreted units.
3. It can be linked to a separately observable economic outcome using consistent entities, periods, geographies, cohorts or accounting units.
4. The relationship does not require invented allocations or an unsupported proxy substitution.
5. There is a plausible route to determining when the information became public.
6. The proposed inference is relevant to a financially meaningful listed-company exposure.

Passing M does **not** demonstrate that the variable predicts anything.

It merely establishes that the proposed relationship is measurable enough to justify deeper research.

Preserve the existing hierarchy:

- **Level A:** Honest retrospective economic evidence.
- **Level B:** Historically actionable information, genuinely available at the relevant decision time.
- **Level C:** Execution-aware investment evidence.

These levels must remain independently gated.

If the proposed Measurement Gate needs refinement after reviewing the actual architecture, explain the refinement and implement the better version rather than copying the PDF mechanically.

## What makes a strong candidate?

A strong candidate combines:

**Economic importance:** The potential financial consequence is credible and meaningful relative to a listed company.

**Distinctive inference:** The relationship contains information beyond a conventional headline, sector factor, commodity price or obvious company announcement.

**Actual observability:** The relevant measurements can be demonstrated from original historical records.

**Independent outcome:** The mechanism's economic consequence can be checked without relying initially on stock returns.

**Information advantage:** There is a plausible reason the implication might not already be fully reflected in prices.

**Time alignment:** Information may affect investor expectations on approximately a 5–20-trading-day horizon.

**Competitive plausibility:** The inference is not clearly redundant with widely available institutional analysis, or it adds a specific measurable increment.

**Retail feasibility:** A realistic individual researcher could acquire the data, reconstruct the logic and potentially implement a strategy without institutional-only access.

**Research efficiency:** Important uncertainty can be eliminated through cheap, decisive measurements.

A candidate must not compensate for an absent distinctive variable simply by scoring highly on originality.

# 6. Use actual evidence, not only literature summaries

For each candidate, create a concise **measurement card** containing:

1. **Candidate ID and mechanism name.**
2. **Economic event or constraint.**
3. **Why it matters financially.**
4. **The specific listed-company exposure that would make it material.**
5. **The exact distinctive predictor**, including units and required granularity.
6. **An original historical source** potentially containing that predictor.
7. **A positive measurement control:** What actual record would demonstrate that the distinctive variable exists?
8. **Independent economic outcome:** What would change if the mechanism were real?
9. **Unit matching:** Can predictor and outcome be matched without unsupported allocations?
10. **Four information clocks:** Economic occurrence, first public availability, dataset timestamp, and research retrieval/reconstruction.
11. **5–20-session expectation-revision mechanism.**
12. **Closest published, commercial, issuer or obvious-market substitute.**
13. **The specific inference that might remain unincorporated.**
14. **Expected data-access and historical-version problems.**
15. **Cheapest decisive falsification.**
16. **Reasons to reject, hold or verify.**
17. **Exact evidence gathered**, distinguishing observed source fields from hypothesised fields.

For an original-source measurement, provide an exact URL or archival locator, historical date, relevant passage or field name, and the applicable units.

Do not say a variable is observable simply because an agency publishes data relating to that industry.

Examples of unacceptable substitutions:

- Treating vessel presence as proof of spare treatment capacity.
- Treating regulatory approval as proof of available manufacturing capacity.
- Treating an agreement's existence as proof of the protected monetary exposure.
- Treating a rail-service deterioration metric as proof that a specific listed company's plant depends on that corridor.
- Treating annual credit balances as proof of usable credits in a specific assessment cohort.
- Treating a modern historical-data reconstruction as proof of original public availability.

If the distinctive variable has not been observed, say **UNVERIFIED**.

Do not promote it through optimistic inference.

# 7. Separate the economic and investment questions

For every serious candidate, distinguish three propositions:

**Economic proposition:** Does the distinctive variable influence a real business or financial outcome?

**Information proposition:** Could an investigator have observed the variable before the relevant outcome or broader market recognition?

**Investment proposition:** Is there a plausible residual stock-price adjustment after the information becomes available, and could it survive costs?

A correct economic prediction does not imply investment alpha.

A public information source does not imply that the market has ignored it.

A complex inference is not automatically a competitive advantage.

Before advancing a candidate, identify:

- What investors likely already know.
- What the specific new information would change.
- Why the implication is not trivial.
- When that implication could become priced.
- Whether the proposed 5–20-day horizon is economically natural.
- Which simple benchmark or alternative interpretation could explain the same effect.

A mechanism that only becomes economically important over several years should not be a leading short-horizon candidate unless there is a concrete event that could change near-term valuation expectations.

Do not inspect equity-return outcomes to justify or rescue an unverified economic hypothesis.

If the competitive situation is unknowable, say so rather than awarding a novelty bonus.

# 10. Candidate selection and hard stopping conditions

Use the following possible dispositions:

**REJECT:** The distinctive proposition fails economically, observationally, competitively or structurally.

**HOLD:** The mechanism may be interesting but a decisive measurement or time-horizon problem remains.

**VERIFY:** A precisely specified, cheap additional observation could resolve the main uncertainty.

**M PASSED:** A real historical example demonstrates the distinctive predictor and an independently matchable economic outcome route.

**LEVEL A DESIGN CANDIDATE:** Measurement is established, economic importance and population design are credible, and a prospective economic investigation is worth considering.

Passing M is not permission to launch Level A.

No candidate should receive a Level A designation merely because other candidates were weaker.

Accept that the correct outcome may be:

**No candidate currently qualifies for deeper investigation.**

Any candidate whose proposed predictor is replaced by a proxy, whose population is changed to avoid missing observations, or whose economic mechanism is materially reformulated must be recorded as a new version of the claim.

Never retrospectively erase an unsuccessful formulation.

# 13. Final scientific standard

This project must remain sceptical, falsifiable and economically grounded.

The goal is **not** to prove that Research OS is special.

The goal is to identify whether there is a small but genuine information advantage worth investigating.

Remember the lessons from our previous investigations:

- An unusual mechanism is not necessarily an unusual signal.
- An unusual signal is not necessarily measurable.
- A measurable signal is not necessarily historically actionable.
- Historically actionable information is not necessarily unpriced.
- A statistical relationship is not necessarily profitable.
- A profitable historical backtest is not necessarily a real-world trading edge.

Our research should eventually answer:

**Can we uncover a publicly observable, economically important relationship between companies, assets, constraints or events that produces genuinely incremental information before the market fully adjusts—and can a small investor exploit that advantage after realistic costs?**

For this task, we are not trying to answer that entire question.

We are trying to identify the **best scientifically justified next measurement** on the way to answering it.

Be independent, ambitious, and prepared to reject every candidate.

**Begin by auditing the repository and attached report, then conduct the bounded discovery investigation and deliver the evidence-grounded findings.**
