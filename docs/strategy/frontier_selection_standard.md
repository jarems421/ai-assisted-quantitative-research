## What each candidate must explain

| Assessment | Evidence required and limit |
| --- | --- |
| **Economic relevance** | Explain the mechanism and independent supporting observations or research. Separate evidence for the broad mechanism from evidence for our exact predictive relationship. A plausible story alone is insufficient. |
| **External interest** | Name the researcher, agency, industry user or provider and the information/product involved. Distinguish advertised offerings, documented customer use and verified purchases. Interest can support relevance; a product listing does not establish demand, investment profitability or our access rights. |
| **Specific-signal saturation** | Identify the nearest published method and closest commercial substitute: population, transformation, target, horizon, timing and exposure mapping. Category familiarity is not proof that our exact rule is traded. Unknown private usage stays UNKNOWN, never presumed low. |
| **Potential differentiation** | State precisely what could add information, why existing users might miss it, the evidence for that friction, and a cheap falsification. A different percentile or model label is not a demonstrated advantage. Implementation difficulty by itself proves neither relevance nor defensibility. |
| **Economic/investment usefulness** | Connect the measurement to a measurable cost, risk, business outcome or market response. Then explain what would be needed to reach timely, cost-aware investment value. A useful operational forecast can already be priced or impossible to trade. |

Assess five separate claims: novelty of the **raw data**, **mechanism**, **signal construction**, **cross-source relationship**, and evidence of a **remaining profitable opportunity**. Evidence for any one does not establish the others. Missing papers are a search limitation, not a novelty bonus. Commercial interest is positive evidence of a useful category when supported, while close substitutes can increase competition risk. Both can be true.

Rank by supported relevance, measurable outcomes, a testable differentiation hypothesis, competition risk and cost of the next informative test. Do not collapse UNKNOWN competitive usage into a favorable numerical score. A candidate without a credible advantage hypothesis may remain a bounded mechanism/benchmark study, explicitly below an investment-opportunity claim. No current candidate has demonstrated a remaining information advantage.

## Investigate a friction before claiming an advantage

For any future promotion, record the closest obtainable substitute, what it supplies, what our proposed relationship adds, and evidence both for and against persistence. Examples of tests, subject to the task's source authorization:

- **Integration/ownership difficulty:** show that a dated relationship changes exposure forecasts beyond a simple join. Check whether existing products already provide it. Hard work alone earns no advantage claim.
- **Interpretation or attention:** show incremental information beyond publicly discussed events and a meaningful simple baseline. Time spent parsing documents is not evidence that competitors miss them.
- **Timing:** establish release/version clocks and compare with available substitutes before Level B claims. Observation time is not an advantage by itself.
- **Capacity:** estimate whether any effect survives spreads, liquidity and feasible size at Level C. A niche may deter large funds while remaining uneconomic even for a small account.
- **Cross-source inference:** state the missing inference precisely and test its increment. Combining two known datasets or renaming a familiar feature is insufficient.

Reject or demote a proposed investment opportunity if its difference merely reproduces a close substitute, disappears against an appropriate baseline, arrives too late, cannot be linked to material exposure, or is consumed by costs. A useful Level A measurement may still be retained as an economic finding. Unknown private activity remains unknown after a limited public search.
