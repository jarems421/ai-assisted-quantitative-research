**2026-10-20. Discovery only; no acquisition or economic tests.** This map responds to the [expanded brief](owner_expanded_discovery_2026-10-20.md), using the preserved [selection standard](frontier_selection_standard.md). None of the previous seven candidates is counted. The original PDF's web, software, automotive/device complaints, cyber, trademark and launch leads are also not recycled. Medicare here concerns payment incentives, and European pharmaceutical records concern manufacturing input qualifications; neither reopens the closed FDA decision-denominator pilot.

Read the [five deep dives](expanded_discovery_deep_dives.md), [conclusion and source-verification proposal](expanded_discovery_report.md) and [evidence/search record](expanded_discovery_evidence.md). IDs identify ideas, not registered experiments or evaluations. Listed companies are economically motivated exposure examples, not certified historical security universes or trade recommendations. Private use of every exact proposed signal remains **UNKNOWN**; no candidate has demonstrated profit.

## Twenty candidate investigations

### F01. Salmon: capacity to respond, rather than lice counts alone

**Mechanism/exposure.** A farm facing lice pressure may need the same treatment vessels as other owners. Congestion could prolong exposure or force costly harvesting, while sector-wide lost supply supports salmon prices. Mowi, SalMar and Lerøy therefore need not have the same net exposure. **Interest/competition.** The [BarentsWatch service](https://www.barentswatch.no/en/tutorials/fish-health/) serves industry and regulators; published forecasts and [Kontali](https://www.kontali.com/products/industry) demonstrate existing analytical interest. Basic lice forecasting is not a new idea. **Possible difference.** Add a separate *shared service-capacity* network to the biological network and dated ownership; distinguish privately controlled response capacity from exposure to rival demand. A reporting/interpretation lag could matter over 1–4 weeks, but specialists may already know more. **History/failure.** Documented health history starts in 2012; vessel tracks have a later start. Rights, actual task identification and historical publication remain gates. A vessel visit is not proof of treatment or spare capacity. [Deep dive](expanded_discovery_deep_dives.md#f01-salmon-treatment-capacity-and-cross-owner-exposure).

### F02. Covered does not mean profitable to provide

**Mechanism/exposure.** A device can remain covered while same-episode coding rules change the provider's incentive to implant or use it. Exposure could reach focused suppliers such as Inspire Medical Systems (INSP), rather than the hospital alone. **Evidence/competition.** [CMS](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coding-billing/national-correct-coding-initiative-ncci-edits/medicare-ncci-procedure-procedure-ptp-edits) publishes payment-edit changes; [AAPC](https://lp.aapc.com/codify/features.aspx) already combines coding/coverage tools. **Difference/lag.** The proposed object is the transfer of a reimbursement change through provider incentives into a manufacturer's procedure funnel, not another coverage flag or code checker. Interpretation, modifier use and billing education may unfold over weeks; sell-side experts can eliminate any delay. **History/failure.** CMS separates posted/effective dates and retains historical information, but current files are not untouched vintages. Coverage, professional payment, facility payment and device revenue must stay separate. [Deep dive](expanded_discovery_deep_dives.md#f02-provider-payment-wedges-and-device-demand).

### F03. Regulatory relief can disappoint the supposed substitute winner

**Mechanism/exposure.** A use-specific authorisation can let an incumbent process continue, preserving demand for an existing formulation and delaying substitution. The same decision may help a dependent industrial user and reduce an alternative supplier's expected orders. Illustrative bridges include PPG, MTU Aero Engines and MKS/Atotech, subject to dated legal-entity mapping. **Evidence/competition.** [ECHA's applications material](https://echa.europa.eu/en-GB/web/guest/applying-for-authorisation/start-preparing-your-application) contains alternatives and economic-use analyses; aerospace consortia already coordinate compliance. **Difference/lag.** Read the decision as a change in the *economic option to switch suppliers*, not a generic hazardous-chemical score. Use/holder chains and technically qualified alternatives could create a delayed 1–4-week interpretation; nothing establishes a remaining market gap. **History/failure.** Dated decisions exist, but complete archives, announcement timing and legal scope require verification. Long consultations can make final decisions unsurprising. [Deep dive](expanded_discovery_deep_dives.md#f03-authorisation-chains-and-substitution-option-value).

### F04. Drug-supply resilience depends on qualified alternatives

**Mechanism/exposure.** Removing a manufacturing qualification may not simply transfer sales to every producer of the same molecule: those producers can share an input site or lack qualified replacement capacity. Dr. Reddy's, Hikma, Teva and Sandoz are possible issuers, not established event beneficiaries. **Evidence/competition.** [EDQM](https://www.edqm.eu/en/actions-on-ceps) publishes substance/certificate actions; [Clarivate](https://clarivate.com/life-sciences-healthcare/training-support/cortellis/product-intelligence-release-information/) explicitly integrates manufacturing and certificate data. **Difference/lag.** Separate genuine replacement options from duplicate legal certificates and map common dependencies; procurement/qualification propagation can take weeks. A molecule-name join is not enough. **History/failure.** EudraGMDP added non-compliance in 2009; EDQM's visible action page is rolling, with historical vintages/customer links unproved. A certificate action is not automatically a shipment ban or shortage. [Deep dive](expanded_discovery_deep_dives.md#f04-pharmaceutical-input-qualification-bottlenecks).

### F05. Meat plants: rerouted livestock can reverse the apparent winner

**Mechanism/exposure.** Suspending slaughter inspection can displace livestock toward another plant, raising its throughput but changing its procurement price; an affected integrated producer and a nearby independent processor may react differently. Tyson (TSN), Pilgrim's Pride (PPC) and Hormel (HRL) are possible product-specific examples. **Evidence/competition.** [FSIS enforcement documentation](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/inspection/regulatory-enforcement) describes quarterly actions; USDA meat-market reporting is a neighboring information channel. The direct documentation opening returned 403 in this review, with no workaround. **Difference/lag.** Species, transport radius, line capacity and reinstatement conditions determine where margin migrates. Industry buyers may already observe this, leaving little delayed equity response. **History/failure.** Action dates in quarterly reports are not daily publication times; plant directories alone do not give spare capacity. Without an evidenced early notice and a dated plant-parent bridge, this is retrospective mechanism research, not a 1–4-week signal. No enforcement corpus acquired.

### F06. Customs exclusions meet inventory already in transit

**Mechanism/exposure.** A forced-labor order can trap committed imports while less-dependent rivals retain sellable inventory. Retailers' domicile or a sustainability score misses the actual shipment/contract exposure. Nike (NKE), Gap (GAP) or solar manufacturers such as Canadian Solar (CSIQ) are illustrations requiring supplier evidence. **Evidence/competition.** [CBP's dictionary](https://www.cbp.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/wro-finding_dashboard_data_dictionary.pdf) distinguishes effective dates, active/modified status and industries; [Panjiva](https://panjiva.com/api-guide/data-selection/data-sources) already organizes shipment data. **Difference/lag.** Use pre-order shipment commitments and substitution qualification, rather than post-event name matches; release uncertainty could affect weeks of cash conversion. **History/failure.** The current order table revises status; consignees, ownership, shipment dates and public filing times differ. Opaque contract rights, bills-of-lading coverage, confidentiality and monitored policy news may defeat the idea. No existing customs/data entitlement is assumed.

### F07. Detained ship, but whose earnings stop?

**Mechanism/exposure.** Detention can move cash-flow risk among owner, technical manager and charterer depending on off-hire clauses. Mapping only the ship's registered owner can get the sign wrong. Genco (GNK), Star Bulk (SBLK) and other listed fleets are plausible dated targets. **Evidence/competition.** [USCG's PSIX export description](https://cgmix.uscg.mil/XML/PSIXExportSearch.aspx) exposes inspections, deficiencies and operational controls; specialist maritime information already serves operators. **Difference/lag.** Combine IMO identity with the pre-existing charter contract and nearby substitute fleet, not just detention counts. Contract interpretation may spread over days, while counterparties may know immediately. **History/failure.** PSIX is a weekly, revisable FOIA snapshot; annual reports are delayed. Detention, release, contact and posting dates differ. Undisclosed charters, tiny vessel earnings shares and rapid resolution can erase financial materiality. No fleet data or inspection rows acquired.

### F08. Visa administration meets a biological deadline

**Mechanism/exposure.** H-2A certification delays near a crop's harvest window could cause yield/quality loss, unlike the same delay outside the window. Fresh Del Monte (FDP), Dole (DOLE) or Limoneira (LMNR) would require worksite and crop evidence; labor contractors need not be the economic owner. **Evidence/competition.** [DOL disclosure files](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/foreign-labor/performance) support longitudinal labor research and disclose quarterly/annual administrative records. **Difference/lag.** The interaction is administrative slack versus crop-specific irreversible timing, not total hiring. **History/failure.** Quarterly release can arrive after harvest; layouts changed in FY2020. Certification is not arrival or actual employment, and mature crops can be served by substitute crews. No daily public status history or dated issuer linkage was established. Interesting operating mechanism, presently a weak actionable 1–4-week source.

### F09. A creditor list can overstate or understate supplier loss

**Mechanism/exposure.** The same gross receivable means different cash risk when a supplier has priority, critical-vendor protection, collateral or credit insurance. A hidden supplier can face a new liquidity shock even after the customer's bankruptcy is known. **Evidence/competition.** [CourtListener RECAP](https://courtlistener.com/recap) provides contributed court filings; [Octus](https://octus.com/products/credit-intelligence/) already offers unsecured-claim intelligence. **Difference/lag.** Seek the *unprotected, material cash exposure* of a listed supplier, not the already-studied bankruptcy-contagion effect. Docket details can emerge over days, but credit specialists have superior access. **History/failure.** Free documents have selective coverage and upload times later than filing. A schedule or claim is disputed evidence, not an adjudicated loss. Listed distributors such as Avnet (AVT) or Arrow (ARW) are exposure examples only if an original case identifies them. [Deep dive](expanded_discovery_deep_dives.md#f09-creditor-protection-and-supplier-cash-risk).

### F10. Gas scarcity follows contract priority, not just pipeline geography

**Mechanism/exposure.** Two nearby power or industrial plants can have different fuel delivery rights and backup options. An operational-flow order may hurt an interruptible customer while a firm-capacity neighbor keeps operating. Vistra (VST), NRG (NRG) and industrial consumers need plant-level rights and hedging evidence. **Evidence/competition.** [FERC](https://www.ferc.gov/fact-sheet-capacity-release) distinguishes firm/interruptible service; [Wood Mackenzie](https://www.woodmac.com/industry/commodity-trading-analytics/oil-gas-transportation-pipelines-storage/) already sells notices, customer information and flow history. **Difference/lag.** The proposed contract-rights/alternate-fuel read-through is more precise than an outage flag, but established desks may already do it. **History/failure.** Public interstate bulletin boards do not establish complete intrastate or old-vintage coverage. Rights, burn, generation and margins differ; weather affects both shortage and prices. Days-to-weeks physical effects are credible; our information advantage is not. Demoted for close commercial substitutes, not familiarity alone.

### F11. Earthquake beneficiaries must already be qualified to replace supply

**Mechanism/exposure.** An unshaken factory is not a substitute if customers have not qualified its process. A quake can therefore create rents for a distant, already-qualified producer rather than the closest geographic rival. Renesas, Rohm, TSMC and specialty-material suppliers are possible examples. **Evidence/competition.** [USGS ShakeMap Atlas](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/data/shakemap/atlas/) provides event products; [Carvalho et al.](https://www.rieti.go.jp/jp/publications/dp/14e035.pdf) studies supply propagation, while [Resilinc](https://resource.resilinc.com/rs/863-OTG-034/images/special-report-reshoring-chip-manufacturing-key-hurdles-and-actionable-tips.pdf) markets sub-tier mapping. **Difference/lag.** Eligibility to replace, versus geographic damage, is the proposed missing economic state. Production substitution can emerge over 1–4 weeks. **History/failure.** Maps are revised; original releases, plant coordinates, product qualification and spare capacity must be dated. Commercial supply-chain maps and rapid issuer notices may subsume the idea; rare disaster cohorts invite hindsight selection.

### F12. A navigation outage matters only when work cannot move

**Mechanism/exposure.** A geomagnetic disruption during a narrow planting window could delay precision-guided operations; an identical disruption off-season may be immaterial. Deere (DE), AGCO (AGCO) and Trimble (TRMB) are technological exposure examples, but farmer inconvenience need not reduce their sales. **Evidence/competition.** [NOAA](https://www.swpc.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/images/Space%20Weather%20101%20Factsheet%20Final.pdf) documents GNSS effects; [University of Illinois economists](https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/fdd053124.pdf) already examine the 2024 planting interruption. **Difference/lag.** Interact operational deadline slack and installed-system dependence, not a generic solar index. **History/failure.** A useful technical forecast is not an equity signal. Relevant severe events are rare and conspicuous; local receiver effects, catch-up work and backup guidance are unmeasured. Days of disruption may have no material 1–4-week listed-company consequence. Demoted despite accessible physical observations.

### F13. Water priority can insulate a drought-exposed fab

**Mechanism/exposure.** Industrial allocation priority and recycling capacity can matter more than distance from a low reservoir. TSMC (TSM), UMC (UMC) and GlobalWafers are plausible exposures, not interchangeable drought shorts. **Evidence/competition.** Taiwan's [daily reservoir dataset 41568](https://data.gov.tw/en/datasets/41568) describes daily reporting, with weekend observations entered later; water allocation is an explicit agency use. Semiconductor water risk is a familiar industry issue. **Difference/lag.** Investigate the gap between headline reservoir stress and the fab's enforceable water/backup position. **History/failure.** The dataset's listing date is not certified full-history availability. Current observations cannot reconstruct past rationing rights, emergency supply or process inventories. Official restrictions are anticipated and closely monitored; production loss can be avoided or appear much later. Watch the rights problem rather than inventing a new drought percentile.

### F14. A salmon bycatch cap can remove pollock supply

**Mechanism/exposure.** An incidental-species ceiling can stop a fishery that still has valuable target-species quota. This could alter surimi/frozen-fish procurement for Nissui or Maruha Nichiro, conditional on actual fleet, contracts and inventory. **Evidence/competition.** [NOAA's management history](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/index.php/alaska/bycatch/chinook-salmon-bycatch-management-alaska) documents the economic closure mechanism; [status records](https://alaskafisheries.noaa.gov/status-of-fisheries/) advertise 2013 onward. **Difference/lag.** Link remaining bycatch headroom to the *downstream product's replaceable inventory*, rather than treating all fish quotas alike. Weeks are operationally plausible. **History/failure.** Industry observers closely track caps; discretionary reallocations can avert closures. Cumulative reports are not original publication vintages, and private fleets dominate direct exposure. Without independently dated contracts/inventory, a listed-security response is speculative. This is a different wild-fishery mechanism from F01's farm service constraint.

### F15. Sponsor revocation can be a counterparty event, not an occupancy event

**Mechanism/exposure.** Losing the ability to sponsor international students could reduce accommodation demand, but a university nomination guarantee may preserve a landlord's rent until the university itself becomes financially stressed. Unite (UTG) is a possible example. **Evidence/competition.** The [Home Office register](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/register-of-licensed-sponsors-students) and [compliance guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/student-sponsor-guidance/student-sponsor-compliance-accessible) document licensing; [Unite's reporting](https://www.unitegroup.com/annual-report-and-accounts-2024) describes university relationships. **Difference/lag.** Distinguish student demand from who guarantees payments, by property and contract. **History/failure.** Register updates are not a complete immutable revocation history; protected contracts are often private, replacement tenants can absorb beds, and letting cycles may be seasonal rather than 1–4 weeks. Industry operators and real-estate analysts may already have better exposure detail. No housing dataset was acquired.

### F16. An orbital anomaly is not automatically a service outage

**Mechanism/exposure.** A satellite maneuver anomaly might threaten service only when the operator lacks in-orbit redundancy or has specific capacity commitments. SES, Eutelsat and Iridium illustrate different fleet economics. **Evidence/competition.** [CelesTrak](https://www.celestrak.org/NORAD/archives/request.php?FORMAT=xml) offers a historical-data request mechanism and operational orbital products; professional operators already monitor conjunctions. **Difference/lag.** Link anomalies to replaceable commercial service obligations, rather than labeling every unusual track a failure. **History/failure.** No historical request was submitted; entitlement, archive scope and stable clocks are unverified. Orbit determination errors, planned maneuvers and proprietary telemetry make operational interpretation difficult. Public GP/TLE records do not reveal transponder health or customer contracts. Rapid operator disclosure and small economic impact can eliminate delayed returns. Demote unless independent service confirmation and material exposure are possible.

### F17. Dealer attrition versus firearms demand

**Mechanism/exposure.** Losing retail licence capacity could impair manufacturer distribution even when background checks remain high, suggesting different exposures for Smith & Wesson (SWBI) and Sturm Ruger (RGR). **Evidence/competition.** [ATF's monthly/state licence listings](https://www.atf.gov/firearms/tools-and-services-firearms-industry/federal-firearms-listings?ffl_date_month%5Bvalue%5D%5Bmonth%5D=1&field_ffl_date_value%5Bvalue%5D%5Byear%5D=2022&field_state_value=OH) and [compliance statistics](https://www.atf.gov/resource-center/data-statistics) establish administrative sources. **Difference/lag.** The tentative distinction is physical distribution loss versus end-customer checks, over weeks. **Why rejected now.** Dealer exits can follow weak demand, licence holders are not all retail stores, and wholesale networks can replace small dealers. No source-backed unique allocation mechanism or superior exposure map was established. Familiar channel/demand analysis plus a new count would not meet the brief; missing exact academic matches earns no credit. Historical listings were not downloaded and timing/coverage remain uncertified.

### F18. The lost accreditation scope can matter more than the lost laboratory

**Mechanism/exposure.** Suspension of one narrow testing method may force customers to reroute work even if the lab stays open. Competing accredited capacity, turnaround time and qualification needs could benefit Intertek, SGS, Bureau Veritas or Eurofins. **Evidence/competition.** [UKAS sanctions](https://www.ukas.com/customer-area/sanctions/organisations-under-sanction/) are scope-specific; [Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/Accreditations/) explicitly describes site/scope accreditations. **Difference/lag.** Construct remaining eligible substitute capacity for the *same test/customer requirement*, not an ESG controversy indicator. Delays could reach earnings expectations over weeks. **History/failure.** [UKAS](https://www.ukas.com/customer-area/sanctions/) describes current suspensions and past-twelve-month withdrawals/resignations, not a complete long archive. Partial/voluntary changes need not imply lost economic activity; many events will be immaterial to global firms. No independent public queue/revenue measure was established. Interesting technical-services lead, not rewarded for obscurity.

### F19. Other insurers' failures can create a tax-offset timing problem

**Mechanism/exposure.** A guaranty-fund call can impose different near-term cash/statutory-capital costs on surviving insurers, depending on state/product premium shares and usable tax offsets. MetLife (MET), Prudential (PRU) and Unum (UNM) illustrate possible firms. **Evidence/competition.** [NOLHGA](https://nolhga.com/the-guaranty-system/insolvency-costs/) publishes accrual-oriented costs and offset provisions; [assessment surveys](https://nolhga.com/the-guaranty-system/assessments-capacity/) distinguish assessed, called and refunded amounts and cover 1988–2024. **Difference/lag.** The proposed signal is the mismatch between a cash call and an insurer's ability to realize offsets, not the failed insurer's default. **History/failure.** Public summaries are largely annual and estimated, with member-level allocation and current tax capacity unverified. Accounting recognition may predate cash, neutralizing apparent surprises; analysts and specialist systems already support these obligations. No credible 1–4-week event sample is yet established.

### F20. Digging intentions can coexist with idle construction crews

**Mechanism/exposure.** A rise in new underground-utility locate requests can indicate work demand, while repeat tickets and late responses may instead signal productivity loss for telecom/civil contractors such as Dycom (DY), MasTec (MTZ) or Quanta (PWR). **Evidence/competition.** [Georgia 811](https://georgia811.com/january-news-3/) publishes monthly ticket graphics and annual analyses; its [creation manual](https://georgia811.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Update-5.1.23.pdf) describes scheduled-excavation and update rules. **Difference/lag.** Separate new-work intention from duplicate workload and actual clearance, rather than adding permit volume to a revenue model. **History/failure.** Public aggregate totals do not establish historical project type, counterparty or late-response microdata; a new 2026 notification regime also threatens comparability. No controlled route from state totals to listed-contractor margins was established. Integration vendors and operators may already see better data. Defer, rather than assume private ticket access.

## What this breadth does and does not establish

The map covers biological production, healthcare payment, chemical processes, pharmaceutical inputs, food processing, customs, shipping, farm labor, legal credit records, energy contracts, geophysical systems, navigation, industrial water, wild fisheries, education/housing, satellites, retail licensing, laboratory services, insurance and civil construction. Some are adjacent industries, but their records and hypothesized transmission mechanisms differ. The five deeper selections were chosen for economic interpretation and a possible information difference, not simply because their endpoints looked easiest.

All twenty were investigated through identified primary documentation or original research; review depth is intentionally uneven. Only five receive extended prior-art/commercial comparisons. For the others, a missing named exact competitor or paper means that assessment is incomplete, not favorable. No available sample count, historical actionability, completed exposure map or 1–4-week market effect has been measured. Negative/demoted ideas remain in the selection history. None of this authorizes a pilot.

The five novelty claims remain distinct for every card: **raw-data novelty** is not claimed for any of these published systems; **mechanism novelty** concerns only the specific proposed transmission, not the established economic ingredients; **signal-construction novelty** is unverified and no numerical rule is selected; **cross-dataset novelty** must be assessed against the closest combination, rather than inferred from the number of joins; a **remaining profitable opportunity** is established for none. The top-three comparisons make their specific proposed differences explicit without upgrading those differences into demonstrated advantages.
