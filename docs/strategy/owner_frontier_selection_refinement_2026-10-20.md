One important refinement to our frontier-discovery philosophy:

I do NOT want Research OS to prioritise datasets simply because nobody appears to have researched or exploited them.

The complete absence of related research, commercial interest or investment activity is not necessarily desirable. It may indicate that the information is economically irrelevant, too noisy, impossible to monetise or otherwise unhelpful.

Conversely, evidence that researchers, alternative-data providers, hedge funds or industry participants are investigating an area can be a POSITIVE signal that the underlying information is potentially valuable.

That does not mean we should pursue heavily saturated strategies.

**Our target is the intersection of economic value, unconventional information and plausible competitive underexploitation.**

I want you to distinguish:

1. **Established economic relevance:** Is there a credible mechanism or independent evidence suggesting the information matters?
2. **Evidence of external interest:** Who is investigating, collecting, purchasing or studying related information? What exactly does that interest establish, and what does it not establish?
3. **Saturation of the specific signal:** Is our proposed predictive relationship widely documented and likely to be traded, or is only the broader dataset/category familiar?
4. **Potential differentiation:** Could an unusual transformation, cross-dataset relationship, timing advantage, exposure mapping or interpretation reveal information existing users might overlook?
5. **Economic and investment usefulness:** Is there a convincing route from the observation to measurable financial outcomes and, potentially, an exploitable market response?

Do not automatically reject datasets sold commercially to investment firms. Investigate what those firms are likely purchasing and whether our proposed information differs materially.

Do not automatically reward datasets with no published research.

Do not treat a familiar dataset as a familiar strategy, or an unfamiliar dataset as an original strategy.

Make an explicit distinction between:

- Novelty of the raw dataset.
- Novelty of the economic mechanism.
- Novelty of the signal construction.
- Novelty of the cross-dataset relationship.
- Evidence that a profitable trading opportunity remains.

A candidate should NOT score highly merely because it is obscure. It must have a compelling economic rationale, some plausible path to measurement, a meaningful potential advantage, and credible reasons that any remaining information advantage might persist.

Also examine why existing market participants might NOT fully exploit it. Possible explanations include integration difficulty, expensive interpretation, fragmented ownership information, limited capacity, attention constraints, or genuinely novel cross-source inference. These are hypotheses to investigate, not advantages to assume.

I want the final opportunity rankings to explicitly address both **evidence of economic value** and **potential competitive saturation**.

Prioritise ideas where the basic information has credible value, but our specific proposed relationship, interpretation or method might be meaningfully underexploited.

The mission is not to find what nobody knows exists.

**The mission is to find economically valuable information that markets may not yet know how to use fully.**

This is the standard I want applied to every candidate in our frontier-discovery research.
